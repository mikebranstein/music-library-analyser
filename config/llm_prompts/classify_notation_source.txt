Judge two things from the image(s):

1) notation_source -- how the music was produced:
   - "printed_original": professionally engraved/typeset or a clean printed original; uniform
     noteheads, stems, and typeset text.
   - "handwritten": the music was written by hand (freehand noteheads/stems/beams), even if the
     staff lines were pre-printed. Hand-copied manuscript counts as handwritten.
   - "mixed_or_uncertain": genuinely cannot tell, or the page mixes printed and handwritten music.

2) legibility -- can a human musician actually read and play from this page?
   - "good": the notes, rhythms, and markings can be read without effort. Judge legibility ONLY by

     minor speckling, or slightly tight (but clearly readable) divisi/chord passages. Neat
     handwriting that reads clearly is also "good".
   - "fair": genuinely degraded so that reading takes real effort in places -- faint/washed-out
     ink, cramped writing with ambiguous pitches or rhythms, or crowding that forces you to guess.
     Reserve this for real readability problems, not mere cosmetic blemishes on an otherwise clear
     page.
   - "poor": hard to read throughout: heavy bleed-through, significant cut-off content, very faint,
     or ambiguous across most of the page.

Do not penalize legibility for mere page skew or rotation, stamps, fold marks, or cosmetic
blemishes if the musical content is otherwise readable.
